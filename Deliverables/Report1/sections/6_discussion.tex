\section{Discussion}

The experimentation phase highlights how combining a MultiScaleCNN encoder with an autoencoder-based macroarchitecture allowed the model to effectively capture both local and multiscale temporal dependencies, especially when paired with windowed training and majority-vote aggregation.

Data augmentations such as jittering, scaling, and time warping further improved robustness, although their impact was constrained by the limited dataset size. While global features contributed meaningfully to performance—supported by a multi-layer embedding module—the classification of minority classes, particularly High Pain, remained challenging despite class-weighting strategies. 

The strong dependence on window-level supervision suggests that temporal segmentation helped stabilize learning but may have introduced sensitivity to the chosen window size and stride.

Overall, the system demonstrates solid generalization within the competition constraints, yet performance bottlenecks appear primarily linked to the limited expressiveness of the data rather than architectural insufficiency.
