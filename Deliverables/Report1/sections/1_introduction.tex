\section{Introduction}

This project addresses the first challenge of the \textit{Artificial Neural Networks and Deep Learning} course (A.Y.\ 2025--2026), which focuses on the supervised classification of \emph{multivariate time series}. The task consists of predicting a subject's pain intensity level from a collection of sensor-derived temporal signals.

The dataset is composed of $n$ multivariate time series of fixed temporal length $T = 160$.  
For each sample $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$, the raw input is a tensor $X_i \in \mathbf{R}^{F \times T},$
where $F = 38$ denotes the total number of feature channels. These channels include:
4 survey-derived channels, 31 biomechanical channels, and 3 global features.
Each multivariate time series $X_i$ is associated with a categorical label $y_i \in \{0,1,2\},$ corresponding respectively to \emph{No Pain}, \emph{Low Pain}, and \emph{High Pain}.  

The goal is to learn a function $f_\theta : \mathbf{R}^{F \times T} \to \{0,1,2\},$
parameterized by $\theta$, that maximizes classification performance under the evaluation metric described below.

The challenge requires the development of a neural network \emph{from scratch}, without the use of pretrained models. Our main objectives are: design expressive temporal models to capture both short- and long-range temporal dependencies maximizing the macro F1-score: $\mathrm{F1}_{\text{m}} = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{c=0}^{2} \mathrm{F1}_c,$
where $\mathrm{F1}_c$ is the class-specific F1-score. We explore multiple architectural paradigms, including recurrent (LSTM/GRU), convolutional, and autoencoder-based models, in order to identify the most effective design for the given problem.
