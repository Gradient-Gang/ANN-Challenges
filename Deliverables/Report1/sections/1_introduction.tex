\section{Introduction}

This project addresses the first challenge of the \textit{Artificial Neural Networks and Deep Learning} course (A.Y.\ 2025--2026), which focuses on the supervised classification of \emph{multivariate time series}. The task consists in predicting a subject's pain intensity level from a collection of sensor-derived temporal signals. Accurate and automated pain-level estimation plays a crucial role in clinical monitoring, rehabilitation, and continuous patient–state assessment.

\subsection{Problem Definition}

The dataset is composed of $n$ multivariate time series of fixed temporal length $T = 160$.  
For each sample $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$, the raw input is a tensor
\[
    X_i \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times T},
\]
where $F = 34$ denotes the total number of feature channels. These channels include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{4 survey-derived channels}, obtained from rule-based aggregations estimating subjectively perceived pain;
    \item \textbf{30 biomechanical channels}, representing continuous joint-angle measurements across multiple body parts;
    \item \textbf{3 global features}, constant over time and describing subject-level attributes.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Each time series $X_i$ is associated with a categorical label
\[
    y_i \in \{0,1,2\},
\]
corresponding respectively to \textbf{No Pain}, \textbf{Low Pain}, and \textbf{High Pain}.  
The goal is to learn a function
\[
    f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{F \times T} \to \{0,1,2\},
\]
parameterized by $\theta$, that maximizes classification performance under the evaluation metric described below.

\subsection{Objectives}

The challenge requires the development of neural architectures \emph{from scratch}, without the use of pretrained models. Our main objectives are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Design expressive temporal models.}  
    The architecture must effectively capture both short- and long-range temporal dependencies, as well as inter-feature correlations inherent in physiological time series.

    \item \textbf{Maximize the macro F1-score.}  
    Model performance is evaluated through the macro-averaged F1-score across the three classes:
    \[
        \mathrm{F1}_{\text{macro}} = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{c=0}^{2} \mathrm{F1}_c,
    \]
    where $\mathrm{F1}_c$ is the class-specific F1-score.

    \item \textbf{Explore multiple architectural paradigms.}  
    We systematically compare feedforward networks, recurrent encoders (LSTM/GRU), convolutional models, and autoencoder-based multi-task architectures in order to identify the most effective design for the given problem.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Approach}

Our approach combines a rigorous preprocessing pipeline, principled dimensionality reduction, and automated hyperparameter optimization. Time series are standardized, augmented with global descriptors, optionally compressed via PCA/POD, and then passed to a modular deep-learning pipeline supporting K-fold cross-validation and model-level windowing.  
Hyperparameters—architectural, optimization-related, and regularization coefficients—are selected through an Optuna-driven search integrated with PyTorch Lightning and a PostgreSQL-backed study for persistent experiment tracking.

The final model is selected as the configuration achieving the highest mean validation F1-score across folds and is subsequently used to generate predictions for the test set.
