\section{Conclusions}

This work presented a complete pipeline for pain-level classification from multivariate time series, integrating preprocessing, feature extraction, a MultiScaleCNN-based autoencoder architecture, extensive hyperparameter optimization, and robust evaluation via K-fold cross-validation.

The experimentation phase showed that the top-performing configuration combined multiscale temporal convolutions, a compact global-feature branch, and windowed prediction aggregation. This setup achieved strong overall performance and confirmed the effectiveness of modular temporal modeling. 
However, the evaluation also revealed persistent challenges, notably class imbalance and the difficulty of capturing distinctive patterns for the rarer pain levels, reflecting limitations primarily linked to the data rather than the architecture itself.


Future improvements could involve integrating attention-based encoders, experimenting with self-supervised temporal representations, or employing ensemble strategies to reduce variance. Expanding the dataset or augmenting it with synthetic but physiologically meaningful trajectories could further enhance performance, especially for underrepresented classes.
